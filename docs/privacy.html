Privacy Policy

Last updated November 26, 2026

Farly is a remote control app for TVs on your home network. It is built to work without collecting anything about you. This page explains what the app does with data on your device.

What we collect

Today, nothing. Farly has no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash-reporting service. There is no Farly server, so the developer never receives information about you or your devices.

Anonymous usage statistics (future)

A future version of Farly may add anonymous usage statistics to help decide what to build next, for example how many people use the app, which TV brands they connect to, and which features or errors come up most often. If it does:

  • The statistics will not identify you. They will not include your name, contact details, location, Wi-Fi name, TV names or addresses, pairing credentials, or anything you type. Your IP address will not be stored with them.
  • They will not be used for advertising or tracking across other apps and websites, and will not be sold.
  • They will be counted in aggregate, and any random identifier used to count unique installs will not be linked to you.
  • This page and the app's App Store privacy details will be updated before the version that sends them is released, and the update will say whether you can turn them off in the app.

What stays on your device

To remember your TVs, Farly saves each TV's name, model, network address and the pairing credential your TV issues (for example a token or key) in the app's private storage on your device. It also saves your custom shortcuts and settings. Deleting a TV in the app, or deleting the app, removes this data.

Your local network

Farly asks for Local Network access so it can find TVs and send them commands. That traffic goes directly between your device and your TV on your own network. Streaming shortcuts ask your TV to open apps like Netflix or YouTube; those services' own terms and privacy policies apply on the TV.

Location (iPhone and Android)

Your phone only reveals the current Wi-Fi name to apps that have location permission. Farly asks for it solely to read the Wi-Fi name so TVs saved at home are not shown on other networks. Farly never reads, stores or sends your location.

Sharing TVs with your Mac

When you choose Share with Mac, your phone briefly offers your saved TVs to your Mac over your local network, protected by a 6-digit code that expires after two minutes. This data, which includes your TVs' pairing credentials, does not leave your network, but the transfer itself is not encrypted, so another device on the same Wi-Fi could intercept it. Only start a share on a network you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi.

TestFlight

If you test Farly through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may collect information such as crash logs and feedback you choose to send, under Apple's Privacy Policy. The developer can see what Apple shares through TestFlight.

Children

Farly does not collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
